# Organizer brief — Back Chris Van Hollen for Senate Majority Leader

## Command and assumptions

- **Command (Nov 13, 2026):** "Do the campaign for senator Van Hollen. Write letter to Senators to support Senator Van Hollen for new Senate leadership."
- **Parsed as:** caucus leadership vote; person Sen. Chris Van Hollen (D-MD); outcome Democratic senators vote for Van Hollen as Senate Majority Leader over incumbent Chuck Schumer; level federal, Senate.
- **Assumptions:** (1) The target is the full 120th-Congress Democratic caucus (55 called so far; Alaska pending). (2) One office only — senator — one letter, since the vote is a caucus vote. (3) The campaign asks constituents to write their own Democratic senator, including the new senators-elect and Colom, even though the action page's live data still reflects the 119th Congress (see Risks). (4) No money ask, no coordination claim.

## Status as of Nov 13, 2026

Van Hollen announced on November 12 that he will challenge Schumer for Senate Majority Leader. The caucus votes by secret ballot on December 1, 2026, and the winner needs 28 of the 55 caucus votes (29 if Alaska is called for the Democrat). It is the first contested Senate Democratic leadership election since 1994. Van Hollen says he has "more than 10" commitments and concedes he is the underdog; Schumer says he has the votes.

## Counter-arguments and fair replies

1. **"Schumer delivered the 55-seat majority — when the manager takes you to the World Series, you give them another contract."** (Gallego, CNN.) Reply: the campaign does not dispute the win; it argues the mandate was for change and the leader should match it. Van Hollen's pitch is about how the majority is used, not whether it was won.
2. **"Van Hollen is the underdog with only 10+ commitments; Schumer says he has the votes."** Reply: the vote is a secret ballot on Dec 1 — public commitments undercount support, and the campaign's point is to show senators their constituents want a change of leader.
3. **"Van Hollen has served in Congress for over two decades, including in other leadership roles, making him a somewhat unlikely candidate to carry an anti-establishment banner."** (CNN.) Reply: his experience is the point — DSCC chair, House leadership, Appropriations and Foreign Relations — he is running on a record of using power, not on outsider status.
4. **"A public leadership fight divides the caucus just as it takes the majority."** Reply: the contest is a normal democratic process — the first contested vote since 1994 — decided by secret ballot; constituents asking their senators' positions is legitimate advocacy.
5. **"Van Hollen said days ago seeking the job was 'not my focus' and has talked about a 2028 presidential run."** (CNN.) Reply: he has said he was listening to members before deciding; the campaign cites his stated platform and voting record, not his motives.
